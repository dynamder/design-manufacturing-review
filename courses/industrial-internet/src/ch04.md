# Chapter 4 · One Service

The network layer held. Data streams out of 800 machines, over the fieldbus, through the gateways, up the extranet lines, into the cloud. And the sheet still hasn't shrunk by one word: the OEE must be *mutually agreed*, traceability must hand over machine, batch, and tool within 48 hours. A bucket of data is not a report, and it is certainly not something the customer will sign. What's missing is the thing that turns data into something worth paying for. The course has a name for it — the **industrial Internet platform** (工业互联网平台) — and its essence is three words: **data + model = service** (数据 + 模型 = 服务). Data is the raw material, model is where the value is added, service is the thing you sell. This chapter builds that pipeline, one break at a time.

## The Senses We Took for Granted

The first break shows up the moment you ask the platform for data. "Data" is doing a lot of quiet work. Somebody has to *feel* the machine before anything can flow. In Chapter 1 the Pi had a few sensors taped on; now we do it properly. That's **perception technology** (感知技术), the platform's bottom rung, and it splits into three jobs: sensors measure *quantities*, RFID recognizes *things*, machine vision sees *shapes*.

**Sensors** (传感器) are classified three ways — by principle (resistive, capacitive, grating, ultrasonic, laser, piezoelectric), by what they measure (temperature, humidity, vibration, pressure, torque), by output (analog, digital) — and every sensor is three elements: a **sensing element** (敏感元件) that catches the physical quantity, a **converting element** (转换元件) that turns it into an electrical signal, and a **conversion circuit** (转换电路). When many sensors watch the same target, you fuse them at three depths (多传感器信息融合): the **raw-data layer** (原始数据层) keeps everything but costs bandwidth and time, the **feature layer** (特征层) extracts features first, and the **decision layer** (决策层) merges only conclusions. Fuse early and you keep detail; fuse late and you save bandwidth.

**RFID** (射频识别) answers "which thing is this" without line-of-sight — unlike a barcode, it reads through boxes, grease, and distance. Its three parts: the **reader** (阅读器), the **electronic tag** (电子标签, antenna + IC chip), and the **application management system**. The physics that matters on the exam is near-field versus far-field: in the **near field** (近场, low/high frequency, within one wavelength) energy couples by magnetic field; in the **far field** (远场, UHF/microwave, beyond one wavelength) the tag reflects the reader's signal back.

**Industrial machine vision** (工业机器视觉) replaces the human eye in four moves — image → extract features → judge → act — and four applications line up behind four verbs: **recognition** (识别, "what is it"), **inspection** (检测, "is it defective"), **measurement** (测量, "how far off"), **positioning** (定位, "where, which way").

Sensing is the easy half, though. Look closer: all three of these just *look*. Someone still has to decide, and someone has to act — and the machine itself has been running that loop for decades. We just never gave it a name.

## The Loop the Machine Already Runs

Go back to Chapter 1's electrical cabinet: the PLC wired to the servos, tool changes in milliseconds. That machine has always been a tiny feedback loop — sense, decide, act — long before we hung sensors on it. The course gives that loop a name: a **cyber-physical system** (CPS, 信息物理系统). *Cyber* is the discrete world of computation and communication; *physical* is the continuous world of processes running in real time. The whole difficulty of CPS is aligning those two clocks — sampling a continuous world into discrete steps. Its essence is a four-step closed loop: **sense the state → analyze in real time → decide scientifically → execute precisely** (状态感知、实时分析、科学决策、精准执行). Skip any step and you get a pile of data, a conclusion that never lands, or a loop broken at the last metre.

CPS comes in three sizes, and the size is the whole point:

| Level | Scope | Examples | The words that matter |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Unit (单元级) | One device, one loop | A **PLC** control unit, a smart CNC | perceptible, computable, self-deciding |
| System (系统级) | Many units networked into a line | **DCS**, **SCADA**, a smart production line | self-organizing, self-optimizing |
| System-of-systems (SoS 级) | Across systems and platforms | **Industrial cloud**, **big-data platform** | same vision as the industrial Internet itself |

The three controllers on the floor map onto these levels, and the exam loves the distinctions. **PLC** (可编程逻辑控制器) is a unit-level CPS; it works by **cyclic scan** (循环扫描) — input sampling → program execution → output refresh, one **scan period** — with an **interrupt** (中断) bolted on for emergencies that can't wait a whole cycle. **DCS** (分布式控制系统) is a system-level CPS built on *dispersed control, centralized management* (分散控制、集中管理), four parts (field control station, operator station, engineer station, system network), three levels (control, monitoring, management). **SCADA** (数据采集与监视控制) is also system-level, but for points scattered over huge distances — one master station, many sub-stations, and the rule *centralized management, dispersed control* (管理集中、控制分散), so that even if the link to the master dies, the field keeps running.

The machine closes its own loop. Now the whole *fleet* generates a flood of data from three different worlds, and that's the next wall.

## A Flood with Three Rivers

The data pouring into the platform isn't one kind of data. It's three (工业大数据, three sources): **enterprise informatization data** (企业信息化数据) from ERP, PLM, SCM, CRM — tidy, structured, slow; **industrial IoT data** (工业物联网数据) from sensors and machines — high-frequency, real-time, messy; and **external cross-domain data** (外部跨界数据) — weather, markets, prices. Three rivers, three temperaments, one analysis. That's **industrial big data** (工业大数据).

It carries the usual big-data "4V" — Volume, Variety, Value (sparse), Velocity — but five more traits separate it from internet big data: **multi-modal** (多模态, mechanical/electromagnetic/thermal/acoustic at once), **cross-scale** (跨尺度, from millisecond to hourly, from device to factory), **high-throughput** (高通量, PB or EB, 24×7), **mechanism-heavy** (重机理, there is real physics underneath — respect causality, not just correlation), and **strongly-correlated** (强关联, part → process → product → environment). That fourth one is the watershed: an internet company can let the data "speak for itself"; a factory has too few critical samples and too much known physics. That's why analysis splits into **knowledge-driven** (知识驱动, mechanism, theory, expert rules — few samples, explainable) and **data-driven** (数据驱动, algorithms, machine learning — finds what humans miss, needs clean data at scale), and real practice mixes both.

The pipeline that turns the flood into decisions is four layers (大数据功能体系架构): **acquisition & exchange → integration & processing → modeling & analysis → decision & control** (采集与交换 → 集成与处理 → 建模与分析 → 决策与控制). Translated: pull it in, clean and store it, build models and compute, take the conclusion and act.

But here's the trouble nobody saw coming: all this computing has to happen *somewhere*, and "somewhere" is the wrong question.

## The Brain Is Too Far Away

The natural reflex is "send it all to the cloud." The cloud (云计算) rents compute and storage by usage, three ways: **IaaS** (infrastructure — rent bare machines, build everything above), **PaaS** (platform — rent a runtime, write only your app), **SaaS** (software — rent a finished function, open and use). Deployed four ways: **public, private, community, hybrid** (公有/私有/社区/混合) — with the caution that a private cloud, being all yours, loses the one thing clouds are for: *elasticity*.

But the cloud has a physics problem the factory can't ignore. A spindle alarm must trip the local stop in milliseconds; a round trip to the cloud — even a fast one — is too slow, too bandwidth-hungry, and ships the cloud a lot of noise it never asked to see. So the platform splits the work in two. **Edge computing** (边缘计算) gathers network, compute, storage, application, *and* intelligence into the edge, right next to the machines, delivering nearby service. The division of labour (边云协同) is the spinal reflex versus the brain: the edge handles real-time, short-cycle jobs and feeds summaries up; the cloud handles long-cycle, whole-fleet analysis and pushes models back down — collaborating at all three layers, IaaS (resources), PaaS (data and intelligence), SaaS (services).

Even with that split, one gap remains: the customer asks "when will this machine fail?", and a pile of historical logs can't answer that on its own. It needs a *mirror*.

## A Mirror That Runs Ahead of the Machine

You want to predict a failure before it happens. That means a model of the machine that evolves *with* it, in real time, so you can run "what if" against tomorrow instead of yesterday. That's the **digital twin** (数字孪生): a digital model of a physical object that updates from the object's live data and stays in sync across its whole life. The idea was sketched by David Gelernter in 1991; the term itself was first used by NASA in November 2010.

Three features decide whether a model is a twin or just a picture: **real-time synchronization** (实时同步), **bidirectional interaction** (双向交互 — it feeds back into the real object, not just observes it), and **full-lifecycle mapping** (全生命周期映射). Miss any one and you have offline simulation, or a 3-D display, or a design model — not a twin. Three nested concepts clear up the vocabulary: the **digital twin (model)** is the virtual representation; the **digital twin system** adds the physical object and its data; the **digital twin technology** is everything that builds, evolves, and runs that system. Siemens names three flavors, and their order is a factory's lifecycle: the **product twin** (产品), the **production/process twin** (工艺/生产 — verify how to build it before you build it), and the **performance/factory twin** (工厂/性能 — keep the built factory running well).

At this point the platform is a full stack. The last question is the most practical one: do we *build* it, or do we *buy* it?

## Build or Buy: The Platform Shelf

You could build. But the giants already have. The course puts three on the table, and the trick is reading their architectures:

- **GE Predix** (2013) — three layers: **edge** (Predix Machine / Connect / edge manager), **cloud platform** (Predix Cloud + Predix.io), **application services** (industrial microservices).
- **Siemens MindSphere** (2016) — an "open, cloud-based IoT operating system," three layers: **application**, **open PaaS**, **connection** (MindConnect Nano / IoT 2040). Note it has no IaaS of its own — it runs on AWS, Azure, Aliyun.
- **航天云网 INDICS** (2015, global launch 2017) — the honest four layers: **edge, IaaS, PaaS, application**, under a "cloud manufacturing" (云制造) banner.

Strip them down and they agree on two things (共通性): the platform's essence is an **open, cloud-based IoT operating system**, and the technical architecture has converged on edge → IaaS → industrial PaaS → industrial SaaS. Three positions follow: the **edge layer is the premise** (it makes physical data visible), **IaaS is the base** (virtualized compute, network, storage), and **industrial PaaS is the core** (it holds the industrial knowledge and algorithms, facing down to devices and up to apps). The differences (差异性) are just as telling: China and the US/Germany differ in industrial know-how and data-collection maturity; each has its own ICT strengths; and China holds the largest market demand and the most complete internet ecosystem.

Now, with the stack assembled, the real question surfaces: what does the platform actually *do* for us?

## Three Ways to Sell a Machine's Data

The platform's whole point is to deliver services, and those services point in three directions (平台应用三个方向), separated by *whose territory they optimize*:

**Smart manufacturing** (智能制造) optimizes inside one factory. Five scenarios: production planning & simulation, flexible production control, total quality management, predictive maintenance (the digital twin's day job), and energy control. Recall the sheet — OEE and 48-hour traceability live right here, in quality management and predictive maintenance.

**Networked collaborative manufacturing** (网络协同制造) pushes past the enterprise wall. Five scenarios: collaborative design, cross-enterprise production, supply-chain collaboration, socialized manufacturing, and service-oriented manufacturing — the last one being how the 800 machines at customer sites stop being a cost and become a recurring "product + service" business.

**Cloud manufacturing** (云制造) is the Chinese-born synthesis: it virtualizes and service-izes manufacturing *resources and capabilities* into a cloud pool, so anyone can draw on them on demand. Its three applications: **cloud design** (云设计), **cloud production** (云生产), and **cloud services** (云服务) for supply, marketing, and after-sales. The keyword to remember: *deep fusion of digitalization, networking, and intelligence* (数字化、网络化、智能化深度融合).

## Wrapping Up

The platform is standing: edge perception feeds data up; CPS closes the loops at the machine; big data turns the flood into models; the edge/cloud split makes it fast where it must be fast; the digital twin runs ahead of failure; and the whole thing gets sold as smart manufacturing, collaborative manufacturing, or cloud manufacturing. Data + model = service, end to end.

And that is exactly when the phone rings. It's the call from Chapter 1, the one we've been too busy to answer: a peer's factory just got hit by ransomware that crawled in through a networked machine and locked the whole line. Every door we opened — 90% connectivity, remote visibility, the customer site on 5G, the cloud, the platform — is a door facing outward. We built network and platform; we never built the third leg of the blueprint. Security. That's the next chapter.
